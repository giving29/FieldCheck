FieldCheck × LOVB
The Business Arrangement

Five ways to structure it. One smart sequence.

There are five ways to build the FieldCheck × LOVB relationship. They aren't mutually exclusive — the smart play sequences them.

Landing LOVB matters more than monetizing LOVB — right now.
Option ALOVB as a paying customer
A recurring fee — a league-wide intelligence license, or per-club subscriptions as it scales.
Why it's good
Cleanest. Real recurring revenue proves the model, funds growth, keeps FieldCheck independent — and recurring revenue is exactly what drives a high exit multiple later.
The trade-off
Slower cash up front; a young league may be budget-conscious early.
Option BLOVB invests in FieldCheck
LOVB or its backers put capital in for a minority stake.
Why it's good
Capital + deep alignment — they want us to win. A league investing is powerful validation for other investors.
The trade-off
Gives up equity; a strategic on the cap table can complicate future acquirers. Best kept minority and clean, if done at all.
Option CRevenue share
FieldCheck takes a cut of the value it drives — incremental ticket sales, sponsorship, fan products tied to FieldCheck features.
Why it's good
Aligns us to outcomes, easy for LOVB to say yes (paid from upside), lucrative if the ticket-sales thesis is real.
The trade-off
Attribution is hard, revenue less predictable. Best as a kicker on top — not the core model.
Option DDesign partner
LOVB gets it low or no cost now; we get the roster, the case study, the logo, the proof. Monetize later.
Why it's good
Removes all friction to landing LOVB — the real near-term goal. The logo + case study are worth more than early cash right now.
The trade-off
Leaves money on the table short-term; must set paid-conversion terms up front so "free" has an expiration.
Option EThe Hybrid — recommended
Sequence them — the best of all four, in order.
1
Design partner now — land LOVB, get the roster, ship the case study, lock the logo. Set paid-conversion terms in writing now.
2
Convert to a paid license — once value is proven, a real league license + per-club as it expands.
3
Add a revenue-share kicker — on fan / ticket products where value is attributable.
4
Keep the door open to a small, clean strategic investment — only if terms don't spook future acquirers.
The one hard line — independence

FieldCheck's whole thesis is the independent, cross-league intelligence layer — every league, every sport. No single league takes a big stake cheaply or locks exclusivity. A league using us is better than a league owning us. Independence is worth more — to the mission and to the multiple.

The Guiding Principle

Land it first. Monetize once it's proven.

Right now, landing LOVB matters more than monetizing LOVB. The roster, the case study, the "a pro league uses us" proof are worth far more to growth and valuation than an early check from a young league.

The sequence
1
Land as a design partner. Free or low-cost. Get the roster, ship the case study, lock the logo. This is Sprint 0's real prize.
2
Prove the value. The head-to-head, the whole-league grading, the fan-facing angles. Show, don't tell.
3
Convert to recurring revenue. A real league license, per-club as it expands. Now it's a business.
4
Layer the upside. Revenue-share kicker on the fan / ticket products FieldCheck drives.
5
Expand. Clubs, then leagues, then sports — the same engine, a wider surface, compounding value.
Why this order wins

Every step earns the next and raises the value of the whole. We never ask for everything up front — we prove, then monetize, then expand. It's the same principle as the sprints: keep showing value, and the value compounds.

The Endgame

It takes off on its own — or a giant pays a premium.

The beautiful part: the same build serves both. This can become an independent, cash-generating company AND something a major strategic pays a premium to acquire. We don't have to choose.

Path 1 — Strategic acquisition
A data / video / media / league platform — Hudl, Genius Sports, Sportradar, a broadcaster — acquires FieldCheck as the intelligence layer on top of their stack. What drives the multiple: recurring revenue, a proprietary data moat, multi-league adoption, being a must-have. It pays most after multi-sport traction — when FieldCheck is a gap they must fill or a threat they can't ignore. One league using us is a feature; many leagues depending on us is a bidding war.
Path 2 — Private equity / financial
Once FieldCheck is cash-flowing across leagues, PE buys profitable vertical-SaaS at healthy multiples. Less dramatic than a strategic bidding war, more reliable.
Path 3 — Independent, cash-flowing
The data-flywheel business throws off real cash without ever selling. The optionality itself is power: we can sell, we don't have to — which paradoxically produces better offers.
Path 4 — Acqui-merge
Merge into a larger sports-tech platform for stock + cash, ride their scale. A middle ground.
The counterintuitive truth
The way to sell for a big multiple is to build like we never will.

Acquirers pay premiums for independence, traction, and inevitability. Build FieldCheck as "the thing Hudl should buy" and we make ourselves dependent and cheap. Build it as "the independent layer every league is adopting" and a strategic has to buy us — and pays up — because we've become impossible to ignore.

Two levers move the multiple most: recurring revenue and a dataset nobody can replicate. We already have the data-moat thesis. Layer real recurring revenue and multi-league adoption on top, and FieldCheck becomes exactly what strategics overpay for.

Why this aligns everyone

Every horizon we build — differentiation, the talent graph, the clubs, the pipeline, the standard — simultaneously grows independent value AND raises the acquisition price. The same sprints serve both dreams. Everyone who helps land leagues, close the raise, and expand is building the exact things that drive the exit — so all our upside points the same direction.
